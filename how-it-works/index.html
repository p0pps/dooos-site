Dooos

How it works

On your devices

Dooos keeps a local copy of your household data on each device using Apple's Core Data framework. You can read and change lists while offline. When a network connection is available, changes are sent and received through iCloud. Sync may not be immediate and depends on iCloud availability.

CloudKit

Dooos uses Apple's CloudKit service through NSPersistentCloudKitContainer. CloudKit stores and synchronizes the app's records in iCloud. Dooos does not operate a separate account system, application server, or database. Apple operates the iCloud and CloudKit infrastructure.

Apple Accounts

The Apple Account signed into iCloud on an iPhone or Mac authenticates that device with CloudKit. Dooos does not ask for a separate username or password. A person's CloudKit identity is matched to their household membership automatically.

Creating and joining a household

One person creates a household and becomes its owner. The owner invites other people using Apple's iCloud sharing interface. The invitation is a CloudKit share, also called a CKShare. Installing Dooos does not by itself provide access to someone else's household; the invited Apple Account must also accept the household invitation.

One household share

All lists, tasks, sections, comments, household members, and activity belong to one household CloudKit share. The household owner controls membership and can remove participants. A participant can leave the household.

“Shared” and “Just me” are organisational settings inside the app. They do not create separate encrypted CloudKit areas. The household owner and members granted full access may be able to see “Just me” data.

iPhone and Mac

The iPhone and Mac apps use the Apple Account signed into iCloud on their device. A person using the same invited Apple Account on another supported device should be matched to the same household participant after CloudKit imports the share.

The Mac app can share its local data files with the Dooos command-line tool on a trusted Mac. Selecting a participant in the Mac app or command-line tool records local attribution; it is not a separate login and does not grant CloudKit access.

Using an agent on a Mac

An agent can use Dooos through the Mac app and its local command-line tool on a dedicated or otherwise trusted Mac. There is no special agent account or CloudKit participant type.

  1. Sign the Mac into iCloud with the Apple Account that will be used on that Mac.
  2. Install and open the Dooos Mac app.
  3. From the household owner's device, invite that Apple Account to the household in the same way as any other member.
  4. Accept the iCloud household invitation on the Mac and allow CloudKit to import the shared household.
  5. Select the invited household participant whose name should appear on actions made from that Mac. The display name can be edited.
  6. Let the agent use the local Dooos command-line tool to read and change lists and tasks.

The Apple Account and accepted CloudKit share provide access. The selected local participant only labels who performed an action; it is not authentication and can be changed by someone with access to the Mac account. Keep the Mac and its user account trusted and secured. Do not give an agent Apple Account credentials or automate Apple Account sign-in.

The household owner can end the Mac's CloudKit access by removing that Apple Account from the household share.

Privacy and backups

Dooos has no advertising, analytics, or tracking SDK. Household content is stored locally and in Apple's iCloud infrastructure. Cloud synchronization is not a backup, so Dooos also provides a versioned JSON export and import path for portable backups.
